# 0005 — Test fixtures: synthetic reads over subsampled real data

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-02

## Context
The per-tool tests need paired FASTQs small enough to commit and to run on every push, with
the variants they contain known exactly. Two sources: subsample a public sequenced sample
(e.g. GIAB HG001) over a small region, or simulate reads from a small public reference slice
with known variants injected.

## Decision
Simulate. Paired reads come from a 100 kb hg38 chr20 slice carrying an injected heterozygous
SNP and deletion, produced by a pinned simulator with a pinned seed and regenerated by a
committed script. The slice and both variants, and how they were chosen, are recorded in
`tests/data/PROVENANCE.md`.

## Consequences
- The truth is what was injected: tests assert exact positions, alleles and genotypes rather
  than concordance against a benchmark.
- The fixtures are committed files, so local and cloud runs read identical inputs with no
  fetch at test time.
- Gives up realism: the simulator's error model stands in for a real library's, and every read
  comes from the slice, so the aligner never sees reads from elsewhere in the genome. Whether
  the pipeline calls real data correctly is out of scope here (GIAB validation, #15).
- Rejected: subsampled real reads. They have a real error profile, but the truth at a site is
  only as good as the benchmark's, the region's variants are whatever the sample happens to
  carry, and depth after subsampling is uneven — so assertions become statistical.
